## 1. Start with data quality (before you even judge the model)
Bad recommendations are usually a data problem, not a model problem. Check the **Data Quality dashboard** in the console (Retail menu → Data Quality). It breaks performance issues into 4 cumulative tiers, where within each tier, data requirements are classified as Compliant, Performance Critical, or Upgrade Blocking. Make sure your model reaches at least Tier 2, preferably Tier 3, before doing any real A/B testing — a Tier 2 model quickly advances to Tier 3 with sufficient high-quality traffic, unlocking peak performance, and you should only run performance comparisons after the model achieves Tier 3, since that's its most optimized state.

## 2. Model-level metrics
There are two categories of metrics to track: model metrics (how well the model predicts) and business metrics (how well recommendations impact your bottom line). Key model metrics:

- **Recall@K** — of all relevant items for a user, what fraction appeared in the top K recommendations — higher recall means the model covers more of the user's interests
- **NDCG** — measures how well the model ranks relevant items, since items the user actually wants should appear at the top, not buried at position 10
- **Diversity** — how varied the recommendations are; a model that always recommends the same popular items scores low on diversity
- **Coverage** — what percentage of your catalog gets recommended to at least one user; low coverage means a large portion of your inventory is invisible

You can pull basic model status via the API:
```python
from google.cloud import retail_v2
client = retail_v2.ModelServiceClient()
model = client.get_model(name=model_name)
print(model.training_state, model.serving_state, model.data_state)
```

## 3. Business metrics (the ones that actually matter to stakeholders)
- **CTR** — percentage of recommended products that users click on
- **Conversion rate** — percentage of clicked recommendations that lead to a purchase
- **Revenue per recommendation** — average revenue generated per recommendation impression

You'll need to join impression logs with purchase/event data yourself (e.g., in BigQuery) since Recommendations AI doesn't hand you a revenue dashboard out of the box.

## 4. Coverage check (quick red flag test)
A simple query pattern: compare distinct products recommended in the last 30 days vs. total in-stock catalog. Low coverage, below roughly 30%, suggests the model is stuck in a popularity bias, only recommending well-known products.

## 5. The real test: A/B testing
The gold standard for evaluation is A/B testing — compare recommendations against a control group. Use the `experimentIds` field in your predict requests to tag traffic into experiment groups, then compare CTR/conversion/revenue between the recommendation-served group and a baseline (e.g., "most popular" or no personalization).

## Practical checklist
1. Confirm data quality tier (2 or 3) before trusting any results
2. Use the console's **Evaluate tab / Preview** to sanity-check recommendations look reasonable for sample users/products
3. Track coverage and diversity to catch popularity-bias problems early
4. Instrument CTR, conversion, and revenue-per-impression in your own analytics (BigQuery + Looker Studio works well)
5. Run a proper A/B test with a control group before declaring success — model-side metrics can look fine while business impact is flat
