For a news company, you'd actually use Google's **Media Recommendations** app type (under Vertex AI Search / Agent Search) rather than the Retail model — it's a distinct product configuration built for content/media engagement instead of purchases. Here's how it maps to your case.

## 1. What data you'd feed it

**Content catalog** — instead of products, you'd import your articles: article ID, headline, category/topic, tags, author, publish timestamp, and description text.

**User events** — the relevant event types for a news app are different from retail:
- `view-home-page` — user lands on the home feed
- `view-item` — user opens an article
- `media-play` — user starts reading/scrolling (used loosely for text, more literally for video/audio content)
- `media-complete` — user finished/substantially consumed the article

## 2. Which recommendation types apply

- **Recommended for You** — for the home page, predicts the next article a user is most likely to engage with based on their engagement history; if you use it on the home page, using Home Page Context as the event type gives better performance since it uses additional usage signals not available with the generic option
- **Others You May Like** — for article detail pages, based on the user's engagement history and the current article being read

## 3. Optimization objective — this is the key decision for news

Media apps support three objectives:
- **Click-through rate** — maximize the chance someone clicks into a recommended article
- **Conversion rate** — defined by a completion threshold; for example, if the conversion threshold is set to 25%, the user meets the objective if they consume at least 25% of the content
- **Watch/consumption duration per session** — maximizes total time spent, not just clicks

For a news company, CTR alone is a trap — it rewards clickbait headlines over substance. Conversion rate (with a completion threshold, e.g. "read past 50%") or duration-per-session is usually the better choice if what you actually care about is people reading, not just clicking and bouncing.

## 4. How to evaluate results — news-specific version

**A. Standard model metrics** (recall@K, NDCG, coverage, diversity) still apply, but weight them differently for news:
- **Coverage matters a lot more here** than in retail. If only your homepage top-5 stories ever get recommended, you have a serious filter-bubble / rich-get-richer problem where breaking or niche stories never surface.
- **Diversity** is arguably your top risk metric. News recommenders that purely optimize engagement tend to converge on sensational or narrow-topic content — recommendation algorithms are designed to optimize time users spend on a platform by recommending content people are likely to engage with, and because users often gravitate to low-quality or sensationalist content, purely engagement-optimized systems can systematically amplify it. Track topic/category diversity per user over a week, not just per session.

**B. News-specific business metrics**
- **CTR** on recommended slots vs. organic navigation
- **Completion rate** (did they read past your threshold, not just click)
- **Session depth** — average articles read per session with vs. without recommendations
- **Return rate** — do users who get good recommendations come back the next day? This is often the real north star for a news product, more than single-session engagement.
- **Time-to-stale** — how fast does yesterday's breaking news drop out of recommendations? News has a much faster content decay curve than retail products, so check whether the model is properly deprioritizing old articles (this ties back to keeping your catalog freshness/timestamp data accurate and imported frequently).

**C. Data quality checks specific to content freshness**
- Since news articles have a short shelf life, verify the ingestion pipeline gets new articles into the catalog fast — recommending an article that's already stale by the time it surfaces is a quality failure specific to this vertical, unlike retail products which stay relevant for weeks/months.
- Check the unjoined ratio (percentage of events referencing article IDs not yet in the catalog) — this is especially likely with breaking news published faster than your catalog import cycle.

**D. A/B testing setup**
- Control group: chronological / editorially curated feed
- Treatment: model-driven recommendations
- Compare: CTR, completion rate, session depth, and — critically — **next-day return rate**, run over at least 1–2 weeks since news consumption patterns vary by day of week and news cycle
- Also worth tracking: **topic diversity score** between control and treatment, so you can catch a filter-bubble effect even if engagement metrics look good

## The core evaluation question for a news company
Don't just ask "did engagement go up." Ask: **did we increase total meaningful reading (completions, return visits) without collapsing readers into a narrow set of topics or headlines?** A news recommender that boosts short-term CTR while shrinking topic diversity or completion rate is a common failure mode — it looks like a win on the dashboard and is actually a product and trust problem.
